# Rapid Prototyping

> Rapid prototyping is the fast fabrication of a physical part, model, or assembly directly from digital design data, used to test and refine a concept before committing to production.

## Definition

Rapid prototyping (RP) refers to techniques that quickly turn a digital design into a tangible object so that form, fit, and function can be evaluated. While the term predates modern 3D printing, [additive manufacturing](additive-manufacturing.md) has become the dominant enabling technology because it produces parts directly from a model without tooling. Rapid prototyping compresses design cycles from weeks to hours or days, allowing engineers to iterate frequently and catch problems early.

## How it works

A typical rapid prototyping loop follows these steps:

1. **Design** the part in [CAD](cad.md) software.
2. **Export** the geometry to a mesh format such as [STL](stl.md).
3. **Prepare** the file in a [slicer](slicer.md) and print it.
4. **Evaluate** the physical prototype — check dimensions, ergonomics, assembly, and function.
5. **Revise** the CAD model and repeat.

Prototypes range from quick "looks-like" appearance models to functional "works-like" parts made in engineering-grade materials. Because each iteration is inexpensive relative to hard tooling, teams can explore many variants and validate decisions with real hardware rather than relying on drawings or simulation alone.
