# 3D Printing vs CNC Machining

> Additive (3D printing) builds parts layer by layer, while subtractive CNC machining cuts parts from solid stock — the right choice depends on geometry, volume, material, and tolerance needs.

3D printing and CNC machining are two of the most common ways to produce custom parts, but they work in opposite directions. Additive manufacturing adds material only where it is needed, fusing successive layers of plastic or metal. CNC (Computer Numerical Control) machining is subtractive: a cutting tool removes material from a solid block or billet until the finished shape remains. Each approach has clear strengths, and many production workflows use both.

## Comparison table

| Dimension | 3D Printing (Additive) | CNC Machining (Subtractive) |
|-----------|------------------------|-----------------------------|
| Process | Builds up material layer by layer | Cuts material away from solid stock |
| Cost | Low setup cost; economical at low volumes | Higher setup/fixturing; cheaper per part at volume |
| Speed | Fast for one-offs and complex shapes | Fast for simple geometries; slower for intricate cuts |
| Accuracy | ~0.1-0.3 mm typical; process-dependent | High precision, often ±0.025 mm or tighter |
| Materials | Polymers, resins, some metals, composites | Metals, plastics, wood, foams — broad stock range |
| Ideal use | Complex geometry, internal channels, prototypes | Tight tolerances, fine surface finish, strong metals |
| Limitations | Anisotropy, surface finish, size limits | Cannot easily make internal/organic geometry; waste |

## Pros and cons

**3D printing**
- Pros: design freedom, no tooling, consolidates assemblies, minimal material waste, fast iteration.
- Cons: layer-line finish, direction-dependent strength, limited large-format and material options.

**CNC machining**
- Pros: excellent tolerances and finish, isotropic strength, wide certified material range.
- Cons: tooling and fixturing costs, material waste, difficulty with undercuts and hollow forms.

## When to choose which

Choose **3D printing** when parts have complex or organic geometry, when you need rapid prototypes, when volumes are low, or when consolidating multiple components into one. Choose **CNC machining** when you need very tight tolerances, a smooth finish, fully dense metal parts, or larger production runs where per-part cost dominates. In practice, many teams prototype additively and move to CNC (or hybrid post-machining of printed parts) for final production.
