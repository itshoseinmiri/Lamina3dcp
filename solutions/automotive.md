# Additive Manufacturing for Automotive

> How Lamina helps automotive teams move from concept to functional part faster with 3D printing and additive manufacturing.

## Industry challenges

Automotive development runs on tight deadlines and frequent design iteration. Teams need physical parts to validate fit, form, and function long before tooling is committed. Traditional prototyping and low-volume tooling are slow and expensive, and late-stage design changes can stall entire programs. Weight reduction, part consolidation, and short production runs for specialty or motorsport vehicles add further pressure.

## How additive manufacturing helps

Additive manufacturing removes tooling from the early loop, so design changes cost time on a screen rather than weeks in a shop. It enables complex geometries — internal channels, lattices, and consolidated assemblies — that are difficult or impossible to machine. Rapid turnaround supports faster iteration cycles, while on-demand production suits low-volume, custom, and legacy parts without minimum-order tooling.

## Example applications

- Interior and dashboard component prototypes
- Air-intake, ducting, and fluid-routing geometries
- Jigs, fixtures, and assembly-line tooling
- Lightweight brackets and mounting hardware
- Low-volume or discontinued replacement parts

## Materials & considerations

Material choice depends on the load, temperature, and environment the part will see. Options range from rigid engineering polymers for structural prototypes to heat- and chemical-resistant grades for under-hood conditions. Consider tolerances, surface finish, and post-processing needs early. For production parts, discuss expected duty cycle and any regulatory or safety requirements so material and process are matched appropriately.
