# Additive Manufacturing for Architecture

> How Lamina helps architects and designers turn digital models into precise physical models and custom building components.

## Industry challenges

Architecture depends on communicating complex spatial ideas to clients, stakeholders, and review boards. Physical models make designs tangible, but handcrafting them is slow and hard to revise. Custom facade elements, fixtures, and bespoke components are often needed in small quantities, where conventional fabrication is expensive and inflexible to design change.

## How additive manufacturing helps

Additive manufacturing translates CAD and BIM geometry directly into accurate scale models, capturing intricate detail that is difficult to build by hand. Designs can be revised and reprinted quickly as concepts evolve. For built work, additive processes enable custom, one-off, and low-volume components — including complex organic forms — without dedicated molds or tooling.

## Example applications

- Scale architectural and urban-planning models
- Detailed facade and screen elements
- Custom interior fixtures and hardware
- Prototypes for furniture and product design
- Complex geometric and parametric forms

## Materials & considerations

Choose materials by purpose: fine-detail resins suit presentation models, while more durable polymers suit functional or handled components. Consider scale, level of detail, surface finish, and whether parts will be painted or assembled. For any element intended for real-world installation, discuss structural loads, environmental exposure, and finishing so the right process is selected.
