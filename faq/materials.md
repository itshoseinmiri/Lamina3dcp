## What are the main material families in 3D printing?

- **Standard thermoplastics (e.g., PLA, PETG, ABS):** Cost-effective, good for form-and-fit prototypes and general parts.
- **Engineering thermoplastics (e.g., nylon/PA, polycarbonate, ASA):** Higher strength, toughness, temperature, and chemical resistance for functional parts.
- **High-performance polymers (e.g., PEEK, PEI/ULTEM):** Extreme heat and chemical resistance for demanding applications.
- **Photopolymer resins:** Very fine detail and smooth surfaces, common in SLA/DLP; mechanical properties vary by resin.
- **Composite / fiber-reinforced (e.g., carbon- or glass-filled):** Added stiffness and strength.
- **Metals (e.g., stainless steel, aluminum, titanium):** For end-use metal parts, available through metal additive processes.

Exact grades offered depend on Lamina's installed processes.

## How do I choose the right material?

Consider these questions:

- **Function:** Is it a visual prototype, a functional test part, or an end-use component?
- **Mechanical loads:** How much strength, stiffness, or impact resistance is needed?
- **Temperature:** What is the maximum service temperature?
- **Environment:** Exposure to UV, moisture, chemicals, or outdoor conditions?
- **Detail and finish:** How fine are the features and how smooth must the surface be?
- **Regulatory needs:** Food-safe, biocompatible, or flame-retardant requirements?
- **Budget:** Standard materials cost less than high-performance or metal options.

If you are unsure, Lamina's engineering consultation can match your requirements to a suitable material.

## Which material is strongest?

There is no single "strongest" answer because strength has many dimensions (tensile, impact, fatigue, temperature). Metals and high-performance polymers like PEEK generally top the range, while reinforced nylons offer a strong, lighter-weight option. The right choice balances performance against cost and printability for your use case.

## Can materials be finished or colored?

Yes. Depending on the material and process, parts can be sanded, painted, dyed, coated, or polished. Some materials print in specific colors while others are finished after printing. Share your appearance requirements when requesting a quote.

## Are the materials food-safe or biocompatible?

Certain specialized materials carry food-contact or biocompatibility certifications, but this depends on the specific grade and how the part is processed. If your application has such requirements, state them explicitly so Lamina can confirm a compliant material at https://lamina3dcp.com.
